# Syra Coffee — Low-Fidelity Wireframe v1

## Purpose
Validate hierarchy and interaction before introducing visual styling, illustration, animation or production components.

The wireframe intentionally uses only structure and content roles.

## Desktop structure

```text
HEADER
  Logo | Shop | Find your coffee | Subscribe | Stores | Cart

HERO
  Brand headline
  Short proposition
  [ Discover coffee ] [ Find a shop ]
  Large visual area reserved for illustration / product composition

FEATURED COFFEE
  [ Product ] [ Product ] [ Product ]
  [ View all coffee ]

FIND YOUR COFFEE
  Short explanation
  [ Take the quiz ]

FROM FARM TO CUP
  Source → Select → Roast → Brew → Enjoy

FIND A SHOP
  [ Map ] [ City / shop information ]
  [ All stores ]

SUBSCRIPTION
  Choose coffee → quantity → preparation → recurring delivery
  [ Subscribe ]

FOOTER
  Shop · Help · Company · Stores · App / Beans · Social · Legal
```

## 01 — Header

**Goal:** provide immediate access to the main tasks without making navigation heavy.

Primary navigation:
- Shop
- Find your coffee
- Subscribe
- Stores

Persistent action:
- Cart

The mobile header should collapse rather than compress into an unreadable desktop row.

## 02 — Hero

**Goal:** communicate Syra's personality and move the user into the shopping journey.

Content:
- Brand-level headline.
- One short supporting sentence.
- Primary CTA: Discover coffee.
- Secondary CTA: Find a shop.
- Large visual area reserved for the future illustration / product composition.

**UX rule:** no product grid in the hero. The first screen establishes the brand and next action.

## 03 — Featured Coffee

**Goal:** give confident shoppers a fast route to purchase without forcing the coffee finder.

Each card contains:
- Product image.
- Name.
- Origin.
- Short tasting profile.
- Price.
- Buy / add-to-cart action.

Homepage limit: **3–4 products**.

## 04 — Find Your Coffee

**Goal:** help uncertain users choose without blocking experienced shoppers.

Optional flow:

```text
Taste preference
      ↓
Preparation method
      ↓
Desired profile
      ↓
Recommendation
      ↓
Product
```

The quiz is an optional discovery path, not a gate to the shop.

## 05 — From Farm to Cup

**Goal:** explain Syra's specialty-coffee philosophy without a long corporate text block.

Structure:

```text
SOURCE → SELECT → ROAST → BREW → ENJOY
```

Each stage can later become a visual storytelling element.

## 06 — Find a Shop

**Goal:** convert digital interest into a physical visit.

Content:
- Map.
- City selection.
- Featured / nearby shop.
- Address.
- Opening hours.
- Shop detail CTA.

Location and opening information must remain extremely clear even when the visual style becomes playful.

## 07 — Subscription

**Goal:** communicate recurring delivery as convenience rather than a complicated pricing table.

Flow:

```text
Choose coffee
     ↓
Choose quantity
     ↓
Choose preparation / grind
     ↓
Receive regularly
```

Detailed plans belong on the subscription page.

## 08 — Footer

Secondary navigation only:
- Shop.
- Help.
- Company / About.
- Stores.
- App / Beans.
- Social.
- Legal.

## Responsive intent

### Desktop
Wide editorial composition with generous whitespace and strong visual moments.

### Tablet
Reduce horizontal density while preserving section rhythm.

### Mobile
The experience becomes a vertical task-oriented flow:

```text
Hero
↓
Featured coffee
↓
Find your coffee
↓
Farm to cup
↓
Find a shop
↓
Subscription
↓
Footer
```

Mobile is not simply the desktop layout stacked mechanically.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] New visitor understands what Syra offers from the hero.
- [ ] Confident shopper can reach products immediately.
- [ ] Uncertain shopper can discover the quiz immediately.
- [ ] Homepage does not become a catalogue.
- [ ] Stores are discoverable without competing with shopping.
- [ ] Subscription is visible but secondary to the core purchase journey.
- [ ] Every section has one dominant purpose.
- [ ] Mobile navigation remains usable.
