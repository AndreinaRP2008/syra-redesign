# Syra Coffee — Research v1

## 01. Brand snapshot

Syra Coffee positions itself around specialty coffee made accessible for everyday life. The brand combines coffee retail, physical coffee shops and education/content around brewing.

The redesign should therefore avoid turning Syra into either a generic ecommerce store or an overly precious specialty-coffee laboratory. The opportunity is to preserve accessibility while giving the digital experience a stronger personality.

## 02. Core offer

The current ecosystem includes:

- Specialty coffee sold online.
- Coffee in multiple origins and profiles.
- Different preparation / grind needs.
- Physical Syra coffee shops.
- Coffee subscriptions.
- Brewing guides and educational content.
- A loyalty/app ecosystem using Beans.

This creates several user intents that the homepage currently has to serve:

1. I want coffee now.
2. I don't know which coffee to choose.
3. I want to learn how to prepare coffee.
4. I want to find a Syra shop.
5. I want recurring delivery.
6. I already use Syra and want to access loyalty/app benefits.

## 03. Primary UX problem

The main redesign opportunity is not a lack of content. It is **content hierarchy**.

Syra has enough products, services and stories to make the experience feel long when everything competes for attention. The redesign should establish a clear hierarchy between:

**Discover → Choose → Buy → Visit → Learn → Stay connected**

The homepage should act as a guided introduction rather than a complete catalogue.

## 04. Brand personality opportunity

Syra already has a visual language that can support a playful direction. For the redesign we want to strengthen:

- Cream / warm neutral foundations.
- Strong black typography and outlines.
- Bright accent colours used intentionally.
- Rounded geometry.
- Illustration and sticker-like elements.
- Product personality.
- Small moments of surprise and motion.

The key constraint is **playful, not childish**.

## 05. Product discovery

Coffee selection is a potential point of friction because users may not understand origins, tasting notes, preparation methods or intensity immediately.

The redesign should introduce visual filters and a guided coffee finder rather than expecting every user to understand specialty-coffee terminology.

Potential discovery dimensions:

- Origin.
- Flavour profile.
- Sweetness / acidity / intensity.
- Brewing method.
- Whole bean vs grind.

## 06. Coffee as character

A strong conceptual direction is to give individual coffees a small visual personality while keeping the product information serious and readable.

A product card can communicate:

- Coffee name.
- Origin.
- Tasting notes.
- Recommended preparation.
- Price.
- Product image.
- A small illustration / character layer.

The character is the emotional hook; the product metadata remains the decision-making layer.

## 07. Education

Syra's brewing guides are an opportunity to turn traditionally text-heavy educational content into visual step-by-step experiences.

Potential entry points:

- V60.
- Moka Pot.
- AeroPress.

The redesign should make learning feel lightweight: **method → equipment → steps → result**.

## 08. Physical shops

Stores should feel like part of the Syra ecosystem rather than a separate directory.

A visual store finder can prioritize:

- City.
- Distance / location.
- Opening hours.
- Address.
- Available services.
- Shop imagery.

The emotional CTA can be closer to **Come say hi** than a generic "Stores" label, while keeping navigation explicit.

## 09. Subscription

Subscription is a recurring-revenue opportunity and deserves a stronger value proposition than a generic pricing block.

The experience should communicate:

**Choose your coffee → choose quantity → choose grind / preparation → receive it regularly.**

A simple configurator is preferable to making users decode several plans at once.

## 10. Loyalty / app

The Beans loyalty system is useful retention infrastructure. It should be discoverable without competing with the primary purchase path.

The redesign should explain the value in a very short visual sequence:

**Buy → collect Beans → redeem benefits.**

## 11. Proposed homepage architecture

### 01 — Hero

Strong brand statement + coffee visual + two clear actions:

- Discover coffee.
- Find a shop.

### 02 — Featured coffees

Three or four products only.

### 03 — Coffee finder

Interactive quiz / guided discovery.

### 04 — From farm to cup

Visual explanation of Syra's coffee journey, sourcing and roasting philosophy.

### 05 — Find a shop

Map + cities + shop cards.

### 06 — Subscription

Simple recurring-coffee proposition and CTA.

### 07 — Footer

Navigation, social, app / loyalty and legal links.

Everything else should live on dedicated pages.

## 12. Design rules for the redesign

### Keep

- Strong contrast.
- Warm neutral base.
- Playful illustrations.
- Rounded components.
- Clear ecommerce CTAs.
- Product photography.
- Specialty-coffee credibility.

### Avoid

- Excessive simultaneous colours.
- Huge decorative characters that obscure content.
- Novelty typography that harms readability.
- Ambiguous icon-only CTAs.
- Long homepage sections that repeat information.
- Animation without UX purpose.

## 13. Success criteria

The redesign should make it easier for a first-time visitor to answer five questions quickly:

1. **What is Syra?**
2. **Which coffee is right for me?**
3. **How do I buy it?**
4. **Where can I experience Syra physically?**
5. **Why should I come back?**

If the visual style becomes more interesting but these answers become harder to find, the redesign has failed.

## 14. Next challenge

**Issue #1 — Define the UX strategy**

Before coding, define:

- Primary user.
- Secondary users.
- Primary conversion.
- Secondary conversions.
- Navigation structure.
- Homepage content hierarchy.
- Coffee discovery flow.

Only after that should the design system and implementation begin.
