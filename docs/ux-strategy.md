# Syra Coffee — UX Strategy v1

## 01. Primary user

### The everyday specialty-coffee drinker

A person who wants noticeably better coffee without needing to be a coffee expert.

They may already know how they like their coffee, but specialty terminology, origins and preparation methods can create decision friction.

**Core need:** make choosing good coffee feel easy, enjoyable and low-risk.

## 02. Secondary users

### Coffee-curious beginner
Needs guidance and reassurance. The coffee finder is especially important here.

### Existing Syra customer
Wants speed: reorder, discover a new coffee, manage subscription, find a shop or use loyalty benefits.

### Coffee enthusiast
Wants more detail about origin, tasting notes, preparation and sourcing. This information should be available without dominating the first-time experience.

## 03. Primary conversion

**Purchase coffee online.**

The homepage should ultimately make product discovery and purchase easy.

## 04. Secondary conversions

1. Find a physical Syra shop.
2. Start a coffee subscription.
3. Discover the right coffee through the quiz.
4. Learn a brewing method.
5. Engage with loyalty / app benefits.

## 05. UX hierarchy

The experience should prioritize actions in this order:

**Choose coffee → Buy → Visit → Subscribe → Learn → Loyalty**

This does not mean the other areas are hidden. It means they should not compete equally with the primary commercial action.

## 06. Navigation proposal

### Primary navigation

- Coffee
- Find a shop
- Subscription
- Guides

### Utility navigation

- Search
- Account / app
- Cart

### Secondary / footer navigation

- About Syra
- Journal / stories
- Loyalty / Beans
- Help
- Contact
- Legal

## 07. Homepage hierarchy

### 1. Hero
Brand statement, product / illustration and two clear actions.

**Primary CTA:** Discover coffee

**Secondary CTA:** Find a shop

### 2. Featured coffee
Three or four products with enough information to start a decision.

### 3. Coffee finder
A short guided quiz for users who do not know what to buy.

### 4. From farm to cup
A visual explanation of sourcing, roasting and the Syra journey.

### 5. Find a shop
Map / city discovery and physical experience.

### 6. Subscription
Simple recurring-delivery proposition.

### 7. Footer
Full navigation and utility links.

## 08. Coffee discovery flow

```text
COFFEE
  ↓
Do you already know what you want?
  ├── YES → Browse / filter → Product → Cart
  │
  └── NO → Coffee finder
               ↓
        Taste preference
               ↓
        Brewing method
               ↓
        Recommendation
               ↓
        Product → Cart
```

The quiz should be optional. Experienced customers must never be forced through it.

## 09. Product information hierarchy

### At a glance

- Name
- Origin
- Flavour profile
- Price
- Product image
- Add to cart

### On product page

- Full tasting notes
- Preparation recommendations
- Grind options
- Origin / producer story
- Sourcing information
- Brewing guidance

This creates progressive disclosure: simple first, depth when requested.

## 10. Homepage content rule

The homepage must answer these questions quickly:

1. What is Syra?
2. What should I buy?
3. Where can I find Syra?
4. Why should I come back?

Any content that does not help answer one of these questions should move to a secondary page or be removed.

## 11. Design challenge translated into UX

The cartoon / playful direction is not the goal by itself.

It should help with:

- Recognition.
- Product differentiation.
- Emotional engagement.
- Wayfinding.
- Explaining coffee concepts.
- Making exploration memorable.

The interface must remain professional through typography, spacing, contrast, information hierarchy and predictable interactions.

## 12. Success criteria

A successful redesign should make the following tasks obvious:

- Find a coffee.
- Understand why it fits you.
- Add it to cart.
- Find a nearby shop.
- Understand the subscription in seconds.
- Access deeper coffee knowledge when desired.
